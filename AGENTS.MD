# Visual Direction: Modern Lineage-Era High Fantasy

The product should feel rooted in the classic MMORPG lineage of *Lineage II*: ancient kingdoms, monumental architecture, worn metal, carved stone, parchment, heraldry, and restrained magic. Treat this as a mood and design lineage, not a request to reproduce proprietary artwork, logos, characters, icons, or interface assets. The goal is a recognizable old-world fantasy atmosphere expressed through an original, modern design system.

## Core aesthetic

Build a world that feels ceremonial, mature, and slightly severe rather than playful or cartoonish. Favor dark iron, charcoal stone, desaturated navy, oxblood, aged bronze, and parchment highlights. Use saturated jewel colors only for meaningful states such as rarity, faction, danger, selection, or magic. Surfaces may have subtle material character—engraved borders, fine grain, beveled metal, faint illumination—but decoration must support hierarchy rather than cover every panel.

Typography should combine a distinctive, inscription-like serif for major titles with a highly readable contemporary face for navigation, body text, values, and controls. Keep display lettering sparse and intentional. Never use ornate fantasy fonts for dense text, small labels, or essential actions.

## Modern interface discipline

The fantasy treatment belongs primarily in framing, typography, illustration, and transitions. Interaction patterns should remain current and immediately understandable:

- Use clear spacing, strong alignment, and a consistent type scale.
- Keep primary actions obvious and use familiar labels such as **Play**, **Save**, **Back**, and **Equip**.
- Prefer a few large, composed panels over many small ornamental boxes.
- Preserve strong contrast and legibility over textured or illustrated backgrounds.
- Make hover, focus, selected, disabled, loading, success, and error states distinct without relying on color alone.
- Support keyboard navigation, visible focus, reduced motion, and readable text at all target sizes.
- Let responsive layouts simplify ornament before they compress or obscure content.

## Signature treatment

Use a restrained “illuminated relic” motif as the project’s memorable visual move. Important objects or destinations may appear inside a dark architectural frame with a thin aged-metal edge and a soft, localized glow that reacts gently to focus or selection. The glow should reveal importance, not create a permanent neon atmosphere. Motion should feel weighty and deliberate—short fades, slow light sweeps, and subtle depth shifts—never bouncy, flashy, or game-like for its own sake.

## Composition and content

Favor cinematic scale, strong silhouettes, asymmetric layouts, and generous negative space. Large artwork or environmental imagery may establish place, while interface content occupies calm, readable regions. Icons should be custom or drawn from one coherent icon family; do not use emoji, Unicode symbols, or mismatched fantasy glyphs as substitutes.

All visible copy must describe real product information or a genuine action. Do not add invented lore, character names, statistics, status feeds, decorative pseudo-Latin, or atmospheric labels merely to make a screen look complete. If real content is unavailable, use clearly identified sample content or leave the area intentionally empty.

## Avoid

Do not drift into generic mobile-fantasy excess: gold on every edge, constant glow, crowded HUD chrome, oversized rarity effects, tiny condensed labels, or numerous competing currencies and badges. Also avoid sterile SaaS minimalism, flat grey component libraries, cyberpunk neon, whimsical medieval styling, and soft rounded cards that weaken the monumental tone. The final result should feel ancient in character, premium in finish, and contemporary in use.
